The closest formal match to what you're describing is **Taizé** — from the ecumenical community in a tiny village in Burgundy, France, founded in 1940. Short phrases repeated again and again, using just a few words to express a basic reality of faith that's quickly grasped by the mind but gradually penetrates the whole being through repetition. Communal, designed for untrained voices, candlelit, not performative.

But Taizé isn't *exactly* what you're describing, because Taizé repeats many times with gradual layering — it's meditative accretion, not the precise three-fold emotional transformation you're talking about (statement → doubt → reaffirmation).

That three-part arc is something slightly different. Longfellow's refrain "peace on earth, good will to men" starts as declaration, becomes bitter irony after the Civil War stanzas, and then returns as hard-won faith. Same words, three emotional registers.
